Hangul reference

Tap any letter to hear it. Letters outlined in red are the ones you mixed up on Oct 5.

ㅗ ㅜ ㅡThe short stroke tells you the vowel. Stroke up is o, stroke down is u, no stroke is eu.
ㅛ ㅠA doubled stroke adds a y. Up and doubled is yo, down and doubled is yu.
ㅁ ㅂA plain box is m. A box with two ears on top is b.
ㄹBetween vowels it's a flap close to r. At the end of a syllable it's always l.

How a syllable is built

Every syllable starts with a consonant (ㅇ is a silent placeholder). Tall vowels like ㅏ ㅓ ㅣ sit to the right of it; flat vowels like ㅗ ㅜ ㅡ sit underneath. A final consonant, the 받침, always goes at the bottom.

Consonants

Top line: sound at the start of a syllable. Bottom line: sound as a final consonant (받침). Doubled letters are tense, with no puff of air.

Plain, aspirated, tense

Korean separates consonants by breath, not by voicing. Hold a paper in front of your mouth: aspirated letters move it, tense letters don't.

Numbers

Korean has two number systems, and the counter word decides which one you use.

Which system?

UseSystemExampleMeaning

Before a counter, native 1–4 and 20 shorten: 하나 → 한, 둘 → 두, 셋 → 세, 넷 → 네, 스물 → 스무.

Big numbers go by 10,000

English groups by thousands; Korean groups by ten-thousands (만). 10,000 is 만, 100,000 is 십만, 1,000,000 is 백만. Prices use these all the time.

Survival phrases

For Phase 5. Turn on self-test to hide the Korean; hover or tab onto a line to check yourself. Mark a phrase known once you can say it without looking; known phrases count toward your level.

    Politeness and culture

    Korean grammar changes with who you're talking to. Getting the level right matters more than getting every particle right.

    Three speech levels

    LevelExampleUse it with
    Formal감사합니다 · 갑니다Professors at first, presentations, officials, announcements
    Polite고마워요 · 가요Almost everyone else. Your default.
    Casual (반말)고마워 · 가Close friends your age or younger, only after they suggest it

    Dropping to 반말 uninvited sounds rude. If someone says 말 놓으세요 or 편하게 말해, they're inviting you to use it.

    Names and titles

    • 씨 after a full name or given name (민수 씨) for peers. Never use it for yourself, and never for a professor or someone clearly older.
    • 교수님 for professors, 선생님 for teachers and often for staff, doctors and older strangers.
    • 선배 is anyone who started before you at school or work. 후배 started after you. In grad school this hierarchy matters.
    • Koreans rarely use "you" (당신). Use the person's name or title instead: 교수님, 내일 시간 있으세요?

    Respectful words

    When talking about someone older or higher, some words switch to an honorific version. You'll hear these from staff and shopkeepers constantly.

    Everyday etiquette

    • Give and receive things, especially to older people, with two hands, or with your left hand touching your right arm.
    • Wait for the oldest person at the table to start eating. Never stand chopsticks upright in rice; it's associated with funeral rites.
    • Pour drinks for others, not for yourself. Turn your head slightly away when drinking with someone much older.
    • Take your shoes off in homes and some restaurants with floor seating.
    • People will ask your age early. It decides the speech level and titles between you.
    • KakaoTalk is how people message. Get it before you arrive.
    • Emergency numbers: 112 for police, 119 for fire and ambulance.

    Daegu and Gyeongsang dialect

    Around Yeungnam, people speak the Gyeongsang dialect. The pitch rises and falls more, it sounds blunter to Seoul ears, and some words change. Everyone understands standard Korean, so keep speaking it; just learn to recognize these.

    DialectStandardMeaning
    뭐라카노?뭐라고 해요?What are you saying?
    와?왜?Why?
    아이다아니야No, it's not
    마이많이a lot
